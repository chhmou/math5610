Course description

A graduate-level course on the theory and practice of numerical algorithms. The course develops the mathematical foundations of scientific computing: direct methods for linear systems (LU factorization with pivoting), Newton's method for systems of nonlinear equations and its convergence theory, floating-point arithmetic and rounding error analysis, conditioning of problems and stability of algorithms, polynomial and spline interpolation (including B-splines), numerical integration (Newton–Cotes, Gaussian, and adaptive quadrature), and the computation of eigenvalues via unitary similarity transformations and the QR algorithm. Emphasis is placed on rigorous error analysis — in particular the interplay between the conditioning of a problem and the backward stability of an algorithm — alongside efficient implementation.

Prerequisites. Linear algebra and advanced calculus/introductory analysis at the undergraduate level; an undergraduate course in numerical methods (e.g., MATH 4610) is recommended; experience programming in MATLAB, Python, or a similar language.

Tentative schedule

  • LU factorization: Frobenius matrices, existence, and the elimination algorithm
  • Pivoting, permutation matrices, and PA = LU
  • Newton's method for nonlinear systems: derivation and the Jacobian
  • Local convergence theory: the affine-invariant Newton theorem and quadratic convergence
  • Newton's method in practice: monotonicity tests and damping
  • Conditioning of computational problems and condition numbers
  • Floating-point arithmetic: the IEEE standard, machine precision, round-off errors
  • Loss of significant digits; stability and backward stability
  • Vector and matrix norms; conditioning of Ax = b
  • Stability of LU with partial pivoting; the growth factor
  • Polynomial interpolation: the Lagrange form
  • Spline interpolation: cubic splines, optimality, tridiagonal construction
  • B-splines: recursive definition, properties, efficient evaluation
  • Numerical integration: trapezoidal and Simpson rules, compound rules, error estimation
  • Gaussian quadrature, Gauss–Kronrod rules, adaptive quadrature
  • Eigenvalue problems: the characteristic polynomial and similarity transformations
  • Schur's theorem and unitary similarity; Householder reflectors
  • Reduction to Hessenberg form; Givens rotations
  • The QR algorithm: shifts, deflation, and convergence
  • Special topics (time permitting)

Topics are tentative and subject to change; updates will be announced in class and on Canvas.
